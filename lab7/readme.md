# LAB 7: I2S Protocol

The Inter-IC Sound (I2S) protocol is a serial communication standard designed specifically for transmitting digital audio data between integrated circuits, such as between audio codecs, digital signal processors (DSPs), and digital microphones. It is widely used in consumer electronics like smartphones, audio interfaces, and smart speakers. I2S enables high-quality audio transfer by separating clock and data lines to ensure proper synchronization and minimal noise interference. In applications, I2S is often employed for connecting digital microphones, DACs, and ADCs with microcontrollers or audio processors.

To design an I2S master receiver to capture audio data from an I2S microphone, the first step is to configure the microcontroller (or SoC) to act as the I2S master. As the master, it will generate the necessary clock signals: a serial clock (SCK) and a word select clock (WS, also known as LRCLK). The I2S microphone acts as the slave device, transmitting digital audio data (typically in Pulse-Code Modulation format) on the data line (SD). The I2S receiver in the master must be configured to match the microphone’s data format (such as sample rate, bit depth, and channel configuration). Once the clocks are synchronized, the receiver captures audio data on the rising or falling edge of the serial clock, depending on the protocol setup. The data is then processed or stored for playback or analysis. Proper attention to timing, clock synchronization, and data alignment is essential to ensure accurate audio capture.
